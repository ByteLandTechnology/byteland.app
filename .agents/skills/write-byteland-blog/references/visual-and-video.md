# Visual and video requirements

Use explanatory SVG diagrams, useful tables and SVG animation when they clarify the
argument. Prefer readable diagrams to decoration. Keep responsive sizing, descriptive
alternatives, contrast, keyboard-operable controls and reduced-motion/static fallbacks.

Place the video at the start of article content, below article metadata and before the
first body section. Do not add boilerplate announcing the available external subtitles.
Provide controls and a poster; do not autoplay. Use external English, Simplified Chinese,
Traditional Chinese and Japanese captions, with synchronized timing and no burned-in text.

## Approved identity and motion

Use the current approved original three-leaf SDF logo and E3 monospaced BYTELAND
wordmark. Base colors are #007aff, #34c759 and #ff3b30. The wordmark uses these three
colors as a gradient. See `video/agent-url-policy/` for editable approved sources and
font license. Do not approximate the mark with unrelated leaves or swap in a bitmap.

Use a light background and roughly one-minute videos. Keep bookends brief while
retaining readable holds. The opening begins with a three-color point, grows and
simultaneously separates into three leaves while rotating, disperses, then gathers
with the approved B5 motion. Intersections have smooth liquid fusion. Preserve
continuous motion, angular phase and tangent direction through gathering and placement.
No stop between growth and separation; no fade at separation or abrupt path switch.
Use clearly perceptible smooth acceleration to a fast peak and deceleration afterward.

Form the logo in the center first, then move it left to reveal the full wordmark.
Only the logo docks quickly and smoothly to the upper-left corner with acceleration
and deceleration; the opening BYTELAND wordmark retracts in place. Keep the corner
logo close to chapter-title height, with clear spacing and optical alignment. Keep it
visible and continuously rotating across chapters; never fade or recreate it per page.

The ending brings the logo back to the center with the wordmark below it. After a
brief readable hold, accelerate rotation and fuse/shrink the leaves into a three-color
point. Geometry changes produce this effect; do not use an opacity fade.

Use one short chapter label in the header, theme-colored with adequate contrast.
Remove duplicate sentence headings, right-side metadata and footer information.
Place the theme-three-color progress bar flush with the lowest video edge, no gap.
Leave sufficient space below the title and around bottom notes and optional captions.
Use distinct, coherent opening/ending transitions and shared-element chapter transitions.

## Audio and source delivery

Use natural English narration. Prefer a stock voice with clear licensing; do not invent
listening tests or clone an unapproved person. Add lively restrained pops, airy swishes
and bright short cues synchronized with motion. Avoid eerie drones, low suspense pads
or long reverberant tails. Duck effects under speech; inspect clipping and loudness,
then listen for pronunciation, timing and balance where actual playback is available.

Keep editable animation, SDF shader, narration script, sound synthesis/source files,
timing, caption files, dependency pins and license notices in the engineering source
tree, outside production assets. Do not commit render caches or duplicate frame dumps.
Record the actual rendering method: a native EGL/FFmpeg render must not be described
as a browser Remotion render. Check the installed Remotion version and applicable
license before use; do not start paid rendering or purchase a license automatically.
